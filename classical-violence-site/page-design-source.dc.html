What is chess-boxing?

The hybrid sport that stages the ultimate battle of brains and brawn. In Classical Violence, chess-boxing is the backdrop for a story about honor, redemption, and learning what it truly means to win.

Chess boxing, or chessboxing, is a hybrid sport that combines chess and boxing.[1][2]

Two combatants play alternating rounds of blitz chess and boxing until one wins by checkmate or knockout.

It is also possible to win by time penalty as in normal chess, and by boxing decision if there is a draw in the chess round.

Source: Wikipedia, “Chess boxing.”

The Rules

4 MIN

Of chess

“The contest starts with a round of chess, followed by a boxing round, followed by another round of chess and so on.”

2 MIN

Of boxing

“In a contest there shall be 11 rounds, 6 rounds of chess, 5 rounds of boxing.”

MATE OR KO

Takes it all

A bout is won by “checkmate (chess round),” “exceeding the time limit (chess round)” or “KO (boxing round).”

Rules quoted from the World Chess Boxing Organisation (WCBO) rulebook.

The Team

The Players Behind the Scenes

Director

Kaden Wittig

Kaden is an award-winning independent filmmaker from Broken Arrow, Oklahoma, with experience spanning feature films, broadcast television, documentary and commercial video.

Starting with home videos at age 6, he has since written and directed multiple festival award-winning short films and directed the Telly Award-winning feature music documentary Pillar: Beyond the Frontline (2025). A graduate of Tulsa Tech’s TV Production program, he earned his B.A. in Film and Media Studies from the University of Oklahoma, magna cum laude. See more at kadenwittig.com.

Writer

Jason Ngo

Jason is a passionate, award-winning screenwriter and filmmaker. He wrote the award-winning 48 Hour Film Project short Murder in Chinatown and was a copywriter for the SwapUp show with VI Marketing and TSET.

In addition to his experience as a screenwriter, he is an established graphic designer and cinematographer, and a No. 1-ranked photographer with Maxim magazine.

Producer

Sullivan McCollum

Sullivan is a young producer with experience in nearly every department on a film set. He’s produced nearly 20 short films including Go For Broke, Cowboy, Cowboy, Cowboy, and award-winning short, Murder In Chinatown.

His deep passion for storytelling through production management and logistics drives his commitment to creating organized, collaborative environments where creativity can thrive.

With a hands-on understanding of filmmaking from development through post-production, Sullivan brings both a producer’s eye for detail and a filmmaker’s appreciation for the creative process to every project.

Executive Producer

Abigail Hutchcraft

Abigail is a producer from Broken Arrow, OK, co-founder and Director of Operations at Triangle Rock Productions, and J.D. candidate at Oklahoma City University School of Law. She executive produced the award-winning short Murder in Chinatown and has worked on short films such as Up(LIFTING) and From a Distance.
